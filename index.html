What is QR Scanner & Generator?

A QR code is the small square of black patterns you see on menus, posters, product labels, and event tickets. It stores a short piece of information — usually a web link — that your camera can read instantly. QR Scanner & Generator is a free browser tool that does two simple jobs: it reads QR codes from your camera or from a saved picture, and it turns any text or link into a new QR code you can download as an image.

There is nothing to install, no account to create, and no payment to make. Open the page in Chrome, Safari, Firefox, or any modern browser on a phone, tablet, or computer and it works right away. Every scan and every code you create stays on your own device.

How to use it

  1. Scan with your camera. Stay on the Live Scan page and tap Allow when your browser asks for camera access. Point the camera at a QR code from about 15 to 30 cm away. The result appears in a second or two.
  2. Scan from a saved image. Open the menu, choose Scan from Image, then select a photo or screenshot, or drag and drop one onto the upload area.
  3. Create a QR code. Open the menu and choose Create QR Code. Type or paste any link, text, phone number, or Wi-Fi details into the box, then tap Generate QR Code. Download the PNG and share or print it.
  4. Look back at past codes. Open History to see your recent scans and creations. Each entry has copy and open buttons so you can reuse or revisit them quickly.
  5. Change settings. Open Settings to switch on dark theme, pick a default camera, and turn the scan sound or vibration on or off.

Common use cases

  • Preview before you clickScan a code on a flyer, menu, or product box and read the full web address before opening it.
  • Decode a screenshotPull a QR out of a chat, an email, or a saved photo without taking a photo of another screen.
  • Share your websitePut your homepage, a product page, or a video link on a business card, shop window, or flyer.
  • Wi-Fi for guestsEncode your network name and password so visitors can join with a single scan instead of typing.
  • Contact details on paperHand out a vCard QR at meetings and events so people can save your details in one tap.
  • Home and office labelsStick a code on storage boxes, cables, or pantry shelves to link to a list or a manual.
  • Event handoutsLink printed invitations to a schedule, a map, or an RSVP form without printing long URLs.
  • Personal lookupUse History to find a link you scanned last week without hunting for the original code.

Tips for better results

  • Use even, soft light. Bright glare and deep shadows are the most common reason a scan fails.
  • Keep the whole code in frame, including the quiet margin around the edges — cropped corners can break a scan.
  • Hold your phone steady about 15 to 30 cm away and let the camera focus before moving.
  • For uploaded pictures, use the sharpest, highest-resolution version you have — avoid screenshots of screenshots.
  • Shorter text makes a simpler code that is easier for older cameras to read. Shorten long URLs before encoding them.
  • When printing, make the code at least 2 cm square with strong contrast — dark ink on a light background — and test it before printing many copies.

Troubleshooting

The camera will not start
Open your browser's site settings and make sure camera access is allowed for this page. Close any other app that may be using the camera, then tap Refresh in Settings.
Nothing happens when I point at the code
Move closer or step back, improve the light, and clean the lens. If the code is on a screen, dim the display a little to cut down reflections.
The uploaded picture returns no result
The image may be blurry, too small, or cropped too tightly. Try a clearer version with all four corners of the code visible and some space around them.
The wrong camera opens
Go to Settings and switch between Back Camera and Front Camera. Your choice is remembered for next time.
The code I made will not scan
Check the text for typos, shorten the input if you can, and reprint at a larger size with a sharper printer. Tiny or low-contrast codes are hard to read.
The result is plain text, not a link
Not every QR holds a web address. Many carry phone numbers, Wi-Fi details, contact cards, or plain notes — those are shown as text for you to copy.

Frequently asked questions

Is this tool really free?

Yes. There is no charge, no sign-up, and no limit on the number of codes you scan or create. The tool is supported by unobtrusive advertising on the page.

Do I need to install an app?

No. Everything runs in your browser on phones, tablets, and computers. There is nothing to download from an app store.

What can I put inside a QR code?

Any plain text up to about 7,000 characters: web links, notes, phone numbers, email addresses, vCard contact cards, and Wi-Fi strings written in the standard WIFI: format.

Can I scan a QR code from a saved image?

Yes. Use the Scan from Image page to upload a photo or screenshot and the tool will read the code inside it for you.

In what format can I download the codes I create?

As a PNG image, which works for printing, embedding in documents, and sharing in chats or emails.

Does it work offline?

You need a connection the first time you open the page so the scanning libraries can load. After that, scanning and creating both work without a network.

Is it safe to open links I scan?

A QR code is just a link you cannot see. Read the address in the result first, treat it like a message from a stranger, and be wary of stickers placed over existing codes in public places.

How do I delete my scan history?

Open Settings and tap Clear Now, or use the Clear All History button on the History page. Everything is stored only on your device.
